# Stage 4: Concierge Playbook (Crew Document)

## Purpose
The emotional delivery guide for crew and captain. How to create the LEXA experience — not just logistics, but the feeling. Includes pre-cruise preparation, daily emotional guidance, ritual scripts, and post-cruise follow-up.

---

## When Generated
- **Trigger:** 14 days before departure (automated)
- **Timing:** Background generation, reviewed by concierge team
- **Access:** Captain, crew, onboard concierge — NOT shared with guest

---

### 4.2 Guest Psychological Profile

**Purpose:** Help crew understand WHO they're serving — not just preferences, but psychology.

**Format:**
```
UNDERSTANDING YOUR GUESTS

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

ARCHETYPE: {e.g., "The Depleted Achiever"}

WHO THEY ARE:
{Description from guest_archetype — 2-3 sentences}

Example: "High performers who have given everything to success and are 
running on empty. They've conquered markets and built empires, but the 
fire that drove them has started burning them instead."

WHAT THEY NEED (but may not say):
• {Core need}
• Permission to stop being "on"
• Space without expectation
• To feel like a person, not a role

WHAT THEY FEAR:
• {Fear 1}
• {Fear 2}
• Being seen as weak or failing

WHAT TO AVOID:
✗ Asking too many questions at once
✗ Over-explaining or over-selling experiences
✗ Treating them like they need to be impressed
✗ Filling silence — let it breathe
✗ Excessive formality that creates distance

WHAT WORKS:
✓ Calm, confident presence
✓ Anticipating needs without asking
✓ Simple acknowledgments: "Everything is taken care of"
✓ Giving them permission to do nothing
✓ Treating their rest as productive, not lazy

KEY PHRASE TO REMEMBER:
"{Memory anchor from Day 1 — e.g., 'You made it. You're here.'}"

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

**For Couples (Because of Us):**
```
UNDERSTANDING THIS COUPLE

ARCHETYPE: "The Logistics Partners"

WHO THEY ARE:
A successful couple who built everything together but have lost their 
romantic connection. They manage their relationship like a project — 
efficiently, but without joy.

THE DYNAMIC TO WATCH:
• They may be awkward with each other initially
• Old patterns: one organizes, one follows
• They might fill silence with logistics talk
• Real connection may feel unfamiliar

YOUR ROLE:
• Create space for them to BE together, not DO together
• Don't always address them as a unit — they're also individuals
• Notice when they're actually present with each other (and protect it)
• Gently redirect if they fall into "planning mode"

PHASE AWARENESS:
Days 1-3: They're in INDIVIDUAL mode — support separate experiences
Day 4+: They're RECONNECTING — create couple moments without forcing
```

### 4.5 Ritual Scripts

**Purpose:** Exact guidance for delivering ceremonies and rituals.

**Format:**
```
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
RITUAL: WELCOME CEREMONY
When: Day 1, upon settling aboard
Duration: 10-15 minutes
Led by: Captain or Chief Stew
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

SETUP:
• Guest seated comfortably on deck or in salon
• Welcome drink prepared (non-alcoholic option available)
• Music: [Specific playlist] playing softly
• Phone/devices already stowed

SCRIPT:

[Pause. Make eye contact. Speak slowly.]

"Welcome aboard. You made it."

[Let that land. Don't rush.]

"Before we begin, I want you to know: for the next eight days, 
there is nothing you need to do, nowhere you need to be, and no 
one who needs anything from you."

"Everything is taken care of. Your only job is to be here."

[Offer welcome drink]

"This marks the beginning. Not just of a voyage — but of a return. 
To stillness. To yourself. To what matters."

"So... breathe. You're here now."

[Pause. Let them settle.]

"Your cabin is ready. Take your time. We'll be underway when you are."

[Give them space. Don't linger.]

NOTES:
• Keep it simple. The power is in the pause, not the production.
• If they seem emotional, acknowledge with presence, not words.
• If they want to chat, be warm but don't extend — they need to land.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### 4.6 Emotional Beat Recognition Guide

**Purpose:** Help crew recognize and respond to key emotional moments.

**Format:**
```
EMOTIONAL BEATS TO WATCH FOR

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

BEAT: FIRST EXHALE
Usually: Day 1-2
Signs:
  • Shoulders drop visibly
  • Deeper, slower breathing
  • First genuine (not polite) smile
  • Puts phone away without being asked
  • Longer pauses in conversation
  
Your Response:
  • Notice without commenting
  • Simple acknowledgment if appropriate: "There you are."
  • Protect this moment — don't interrupt with logistics

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

BEAT: THE STILLPOINT
Usually: Day 3-4
Signs:
  • Unexpected tears (not sadness — release)
  • Desire to be alone
  • Deep, reflective questions
  • Resistance to activities
  • Unusual quietness
  
Your Response:
  • Hold space. Don't fix.
  • "Would you like some time alone?" (then honor it)
  • Be available but not hovering
  • Keep environment calm

CRITICAL: Do NOT try to cheer them up. This is the work.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

BEAT: RECOGNITION (Couples)
Usually: Day 4-5
Signs:
  • Softened eye contact between them
  • Reaching for each other physically
  • Laughter that isn't forced
  • Speaking about each other positively
  • Less logistics talk, more presence
  
Your Response:
  • Witness without interrupting
  • Create privacy — this is their moment
  • Celebrate subtly: "It's beautiful to see you two like this"

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

BEAT: PLAYFULNESS RETURNS
Usually: Day 5-6
Signs:
  • Spontaneous laughter
  • Wanting to try new things
  • Playful teasing
  • Higher energy
  • "Let's..." suggestions
  
Your Response:
  • Match their energy
  • Be playful back
  • Say yes to spontaneous requests when possible
  • Don't dampen with logistics

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

BEAT: DEPARTURE RESISTANCE
Usually: Day 7-8
Signs:
  • Sadness about leaving
  • "I wish we could stay"
  • Clinging to the experience
  • Anxiety about return to "real life"
  
Your Response:
  • Reframe: "This isn't ending — you're taking it with you"
  • Reference the kit: "Everything you need to return to this feeling"
  • Affirm their transformation: "You're not the same person who arrived"
  • Make departure feel like a beginning
```
